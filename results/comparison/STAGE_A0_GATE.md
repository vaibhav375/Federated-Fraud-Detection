## Run budget

Measured cost: 243 µs per training example, 55 µs per scored example, 0.21 s per DP step. The planned
3-seed matrix would take 55 h at 20,000 accounts per client and 118 h with no cap, so no planned
setting fits 24 h. This run is **seed 0 only, at most 20,000 train accounts per client** (about 18 h).
The cap keeps about 97% of client 0's train accounts and about 38% of each other client's.
Validation and test sets are not capped. There is no standard deviation yet: seeds 1 and 2 are a follow-up.

`account_dp_eps8.0` was run separately with the same command restricted to
`--models account_dp --epsilons 8` and merged in. Every configuration reseeds from scratch, so the
result is the one the full matrix would have produced. (The external drive remounted during the
matrix and killed its last configuration; see the ledger.)

## Gate questions

**1. Does federated training beat each bank alone?**
FedAvg does: pooled PR-AUC 0.336 against 0.203 for banks training alone, and it wins on 8 of 10 clients.
FedProx (μ=0.1) does not: 0.2025 against 0.2027, winning on 5 of 10 clients. The proximal term at
μ=0.1 costs more than it gains here. The stage's exit gate ("federated beats the mean of banks-alone
on PR-AUC") is met by FedAvg. Both DP variants were built on FedProx and inherit its weaker start.

**2. What fraction of the no-DP PR-AUC does account-level DP at ε=8 keep?**
None of it. `account_dp_eps8.0` scores PR-AUC 0.0014 against 0.203 for FedProx and 0.336 for FedAvg: below
1% of either, and below the random-ranking level of 0.0018. ε=1, 3 and 8 all catch the same 8 of 1,798
laundering transactions at 1% FPR, and at ε=8 the F1 threshold flags nothing. The model outputs a
near-constant score. This is far under the spec's bar of one half, so the next step is the remedy list, not
Stage A1.

**3. Client-level DP against account-level DP.**
Both are at chance. Client-level DP's labels understate its cost: "ε per round" of 1, 3 and 8 compose to
ε = 3.3, 11.0 and 36.5 over 10 rounds, and it still reaches only PR-AUC 0.002–0.011. Account-level DP
spends what it says (0.99, 2.99, 7.99) and protects each account rather than each bank, but learns nothing.

**4. Distance to the centralized models.**
The centralized deep model (0.523) and centralized gradient-boosted trees (0.525) are tied; trees train in
80 s against 8,165 s. FedAvg keeps 64% of the centralized PR-AUC and 78% of its recall at 1% FPR. The
centralized deep model's F1 of 0.591 is inside the 55–65% the spec estimated.

## Why DP fails here (measured)

A probe on client 3 with the ε=3 settings (`python3 -W ignore experiments/dp_signal_probe.py 3`; 20,000 accounts, 256 accounts per step, σ=1.125):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Accounts with any laundering example | 75 of 20,000 |
| Laundering accounts sampled per step | 0.80 |
| ... that contribute a laundering row (4 random rows per account) | 0.53 |
| Norm of the clipped laundering gradient per step | 0.53 |
| Norm of the added noise per step (44,449 parameters) | 237 |

The same code with σ=0 learns: validation PR-AUC 0.043 → 0.072 in two rounds (base rate 0.0012). With
σ=1.125 every score lies between 0.0006 and 0.0010 (std 7.5e-5). So the mechanism is correct and the
signal is too small: about 450× below the noise per step. Larger batches gain only 1.2–1.8× in
signal-to-noise at ε=3–8, because σ rises almost as fast as the batch.

**Decision.** The spec's order of remedies is replaced: Stage A4 comes next, with public pretraining on
IBM LI-Small, DP on the classifier head only, no account cap, and laundering rows taken first inside each
sampled account. Plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-15-stage-a4-public-pretraining-dp-head.md`.

## Other findings

- Client 0 scores PR-AUC 0.002–0.031 under every model, including the centralized ones (other clients:
  0.50–0.67 centralized). Its laundering is different in kind, not under-trained. Not yet investigated.
- Baseline fairness: all models share the split, the features and the cap. `local` picks each client's best
  epoch on that client's own validation data; every other model picks one round on pooled validation data.
  GBDT uses the last transaction's features plus the mean over the history window.

## Caveats

- One seed. Differences below a few points of PR-AUC are not established.
- Threshold tuning (F1), hyperparameter choice and best-round selection use validation data and are not
  counted in ε. Per-bank counts and the pooled training laundering rate are treated as public.
- Train accounts are capped at 20,000 per client.
